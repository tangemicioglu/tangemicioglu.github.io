Brain-computer interface research draws on neuroscience, signal processing, machine learning, hardware, clinical practice, and human-computer interaction. That breadth makes the field productive, but it also makes entry difficult: a tutorial that assumes fluency in one discipline can be inaccessible to a student arriving from another.

As a member of the BCI Society's Postdoc & Student Committee, I worked on tutorials for the BCI Meeting, the BCI Society's international conference, and began organizing the material into a reusable repository. The goal was to make methods taught at the meeting available beyond a single live session, with notebooks that expose the intermediate steps behind an analysis instead of presenting only final results.

The initial tutorial focuses on machine learning for BCI data in a notebook format. It is intended to be read, modified, and rerun, allowing learners to inspect how preprocessing choices, features, models, and evaluation procedures affect a result. This is particularly important in BCI, where small datasets and subject-to-subject variation can make an apparently strong classifier difficult to interpret.

BCIhub remains an educational effort rather than a complete curriculum. Its direction is to complement papers and recorded talks with practical, inspectable materials contributed by researchers across the community. The [tutorial repository](https://github.com/tangemicioglu/tutorial-bci-meeting) contains the current notebook, and the [BCI Society](https://bcisociety.org/) provides information about the meeting and its broader education and early-career activities.
